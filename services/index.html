What we model

Frames, platforms, and supporting assemblies

Steel Structures

We model steel frames, platforms, supports, and similar structural assemblies so the arrangement, connections, and fit of each member can be reviewed before anyone commits to it. The focus is on getting the geometry and the assembly logic right, and on presenting it clearly enough that everyone reading the model sees the same design.

What it covers

  • Steel frames and framed enclosures
  • Access platforms, walkways, and landings
  • Support structures, stands, and mounting frames
  • Bracing arrangements and connection detail modeling

What to send us

  • Sketches, photographs, or marked-up drawings
  • Overall dimensions and available space
  • Section sizes or member types you intend to use
  • How the structure connects to surrounding equipment or floors

Belt, roller, and custom handling layouts

Conveyors

We develop CAD models for conveyor equipment and the layouts they sit in — from a single belt or roller section through to a multi-section arrangement that has to fit a specific footprint. Frames, rollers, drive arrangements, guarding, and supports are modeled as an assembly so clearances and transitions can be checked properly.

What it covers

  • Belt conveyors, including frames, pulleys, and take-up arrangements
  • Roller conveyors, both gravity and driven
  • Custom conveyor layouts and multi-section assemblies
  • Transfers, guiding, guarding, and support stands

What to send us

  • Conveyor length, width, and any height or incline change
  • What is being conveyed, and its size or weight
  • The footprint available and any obstructions to route around
  • Where the line starts, ends, and connects to other equipment

Pick-and-place, packing, and custom mechanisms

Automated Machines

We turn automation concepts into mechanical CAD models — pick-and-place arrangements, packing machine concepts, and custom assemblies built around a specific motion or handling task. We model the mechanical side: structure, motion geometry, mounting, and how the parts fit and move together.

What it covers

  • Pick-and-place arrangements and handling heads
  • Packing machine concepts and product-handling stations
  • Custom mechanical assemblies for automation concepts
  • Machine framing, mounting, guarding, and access

What to send us

  • What the machine needs to do, step by step
  • The product or part being handled, with its dimensions
  • Reach, stroke, or travel the mechanism needs to cover
  • Any existing equipment it must work alongside or fit within

From a small gear to a custom mechanism

Parts & Assemblies

We create custom SolidWorks parts and assemblies from sketches, dimensions, and references. A single gear, shaft, bracket, housing, sheet-metal guard, or a complete mechanism can be modeled with the features and interfaces your project needs. The agreed handover can include native parts, assembly mates, drawings, and neutral CAD files.

What it covers

  • Gears, shafts, brackets, and bearing housings
  • Sheet-metal guards, covers, and flat patterns
  • Custom mechanisms, fixtures, and mechanical assemblies
  • Part variants, configurations, and drawing updates

What to send us

  • A sketch, drawing, reference photo, or existing part model
  • Critical dimensions, mating interfaces, and intended material
  • The part’s function and the components it must fit
  • Required file formats and drawing detail

What moves the price

How many parts there are
A frame of six members and a machine of two hundred are different jobs.
How much drawing detail you need
A model to look at costs less than a full drawing set a workshop can build from.
How complete the information is
Clear dimensions and photographs are quick; working them out from scratch is not.
How many rounds of change
Changes inside the agreed scope are included. A new direction is re-quoted.
How soon you need it
A normal timeline costs less than one that displaces other work.

Getting a real number

Figures are in USD and are typical ranges, not quotes: they are the hourly rate against the hours a job of that kind usually takes. Send us the scope and you will get a fixed price for your job, at no charge.

Send what you have — a sketch on paper, a photograph of the machine, or a drawing that needs redrawing. If the scope is not clear yet, we will work it out with you first.
