Simulation Sanity Checks on Steel Platforms and Frames

Beam elements, fixtures and mesh — plus an honest word about what FEA does not do

Table of Contents

  • Use beams for frames
  • Reading the results usefully

Key takeaways

  • Use fixtures and loads that match the physical structure.
  • Check how mesh changes affect the result.
  • Keep CAD studies distinct from structural verification and sign-off.

Simulation inside SOLIDWORKS is at its best early, when a question is still cheap to answer: is this stringer deep enough, would a brace here help, is the deflection going to feel bouncy underfoot. Used that way it shapes a design. Used as a substitute for a structural check, it is a liability.

Use beams for frames

Weldment structural members are treated as beam elements by default, and that is usually what you want. A beam mesh solves in seconds where a solid mesh of the same frame can take an age and add nothing. Check the joints are recognised, treat them as rigid or pinned to match reality, and switch individual members to solid only where you need local detail such as a bolted end plate.

  • Fixtures: fix the feet where the frame is bolted down, not the whole base face, or the model will look stiffer than the real thing.
  • Loads: apply distributed load to the deck members rather than a point load at mid-span, unless a point load is what actually happens.
  • Include self-weight with gravity; on a steel platform it is not negligible.
  • Run the same study with a refined mesh. If the answer moves a lot, the first answer was mesh, not physics.
What this is not

A static study in CAD is a design aid. It does not account for code load combinations, connection design, fatigue, or site conditions, and a factor of safety plot is not a compliance statement. Structural sign-off is the job of the qualified engineer responsible for the installation, working to the standards that apply where it is being built.

Reading the results usefully

Deflection is often the more useful plot. A platform can be nowhere near yield and still feel unpleasant to stand on, and a serviceability limit — a span-over-something figure agreed with the client or set by the relevant standard — is the number that decides whether the design is comfortable to use.

Finally, record what you ran: loads, fixtures, material, mesh and the version of the model. A result without its assumptions is not evidence, and six months later nobody remembers whether that 4 mm deflection included the handrail load or not.
