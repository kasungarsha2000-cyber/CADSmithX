Sheet Metal Guards and Covers That Fold Up Correctly

K-factor, bend relief, and flat patterns the laser shop can cut straight from

Table of Contents

  • Get the bend numbers from the fabricator
  • Building the guard
  • The flat pattern and the DXF

Key takeaways

  • Agree bend allowances and tooling with the fabricator.
  • Model reliefs and corners as sheet-metal features.
  • Check the flat pattern and separate DXF cut and bend layers.

Guards, covers, chutes and control panel enclosures are all sheet metal, and sheet metal is the one discipline where the model must agree with a specific machine in a specific workshop. Steel does not fold on a perfect line; it stretches around the bend. How much it stretches is what the K-factor, bend allowance or bend deduction describes.

Get the bend numbers from the fabricator

SOLIDWORKS starts new sheet metal parts with a default K-factor, and for mild steel a value in the region of 0.4 to 0.5 is a common starting point. It is a starting point, not an answer. The real number depends on material, thickness, tooling and the press brake itself. Ask the shop that will fold the part whether they can give you a bend table — a table of allowances per thickness and radius — and set the part to use it. One conversation removes a whole category of remake.

Inside bend radius is not free

The bend radius in your model should match a punch the shop actually owns. A radius typed in because it looked right will either be quietly changed on the floor, or produce a part whose flat length is wrong. Agree it, then store it in your sheet metal gauge table so it is picked automatically next time.

Building the guard

  • Start from Base Flange/Tab, then add Edge Flanges — do not model a box with extrusions and convert it later if you can avoid it.
  • Use Closed Corner or Welded Corner where two flanges meet, so the corner is defined rather than left as a gap.
  • Let SOLIDWORKS add corner and bend relief; without relief the material tears in reality, whatever the model shows.
  • Put louvres, hinge cutouts and embosses in with Forming Tools so they flatten correctly.
  • Model mesh panels as a flat part with a note about the mesh spec, rather than thousands of modelled holes that will slow every rebuild.

The flat pattern and the DXF

Every sheet metal part carries a Flat-Pattern configuration. Export it as DXF or DWG for cutting, and take care with what goes in the file: the profile the laser cuts on one layer, bend lines on a separate layer, and nothing else. Most cutting shops will tell you exactly which layers they want. Save that mapping once and the export becomes a two-click job.

Finally, check the flat before you send it. Flatten the part on screen and look for overlaps and negative material at the corners — the model will happily show a solid that cannot exist as a folded sheet, and the first person to notice should not be the operator at the brake.
